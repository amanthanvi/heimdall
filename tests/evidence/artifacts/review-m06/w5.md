# M06 W5 merge candidate review: independent review

- **Candidate:** `25229f74ff5fd84c0ff905e43ac5be12b8f240a2` (`m06/w5`), one commit on `main` `ec985922`.
- **Reviewer:** the one independent review of the wave (ADR-0009 §2 item 1).
- **Method:** I read the diff by risk, following the brief's six focus areas in order. I ran focused crate tests and two hand probes of the debug CLI with every fixture store set to scratch. I ran no release binary, no `check.sh`, nothing on a remote host and no `heimdall connect` or `run -- ssh`.
- **Scope:** the review is read-only. I edited nothing in the repository.

## Verdict: MERGE

Nothing I found meets ADR-0009 §2.1's blocking test:
- In each of the six focus areas the authority boundaries hold.
- **The named-pipe agent:** it serves only this user, only list and purpose-bound sign of K_mgmt, never forwards, and lasts exactly the session.
- **Widening:** no widening is installed without an attended local-owner decision bound to its exact delta. A paired session never produces or approves a local record.
- **The two-key lift:** a lift needs both the runner-local attended action and the controller owner's attended acknowledgement.
- **Requesters:** a requester's request is never signed on its own word.

**Fix soon:** M06-W5-R01 (moderate). It weakens a 2026-11-22 ruling ("the approval names the effect being lifted") on the most common lift path. It is a gap in what the approval shows, not a bypass of the two keys, so it does not block. The orchestrator may rule otherwise if it treats that naming as part of the consent.

The other findings are low.

## Findings

### M06-W5-R01 (moderate): a delivered pause, or a cutoff whose scope differs, is lifted without the approval naming it

**Where:**
- `crates/store/src/ext_effects.rs:953`: `note_lifted_policy_revisions`. It is called from `authority_report` at `:1060`.
- `crates/app/src/operations/stop_m05.rs:282`: `delivered_lifts`, which matches exact `scope_key` only.
- `crates/app/src/operations/stop_m05.rs:887`: `lifted_effects`, which needs a `DecisionBasis::Targeted`.
- `crates/app/src/operations.rs:12060`: `delivered_lift_form` applies only when the runner does not hold the policy.
- `crates/heimdall/src/cli.rs:1988`: `access resume --policy P` sends the untargeted `Resume { policy_id: Some(P), target: None }`.
- `crates/store/src/ext_effects.rs:104`: a delivered pause's scope key is always `policy:<id>`.

**The ruling:** ADR-0012 §7.4 ⟨2026-11-22⟩ "The in-force flag" (`docs/decisions/0012-environments-and-transport.md:679`) says: "…a targeted, attended `resume` or `clear-cutoff` lifts the in-force delivered revisions of exactly its scope … **The approval names the effect being lifted** (its scope, the delivering controller, and when)."

**How a lift can happen without being named:**
- At every authority report, `note_lifted_policy_revisions` clears `in_force` on any delivered revision whose acknowledged policies all still exist and are no longer paused or cut off.
- Any attended resume or clear-cutoff that unblocks those policies therefore lifts the effect: the untargeted per-policy form, or a targeted form of another scope.
- `lifted_effects` names an effect only for a targeted decision whose scope key equals the effect's.
- Delivered pauses are always `policy:<id>`. The CLI's targeted forms are profile, workspace and all (`m05_cmd.rs:1466`), and `access resume --policy` sends the untargeted form.
- So on a runner that **holds** the paused policy, no lift approval of a delivered pause ever names it. That is the normal case, because a delivered pause is applied to an existing policy.
- A delivered cutoff of scope `profile:X` is lifted unnamed in the same way by `clear-cutoff --policy P`, or by `--all`.

**Failure scenario:**
1. Owner controller C delivers `pause` of policy P to runner R. R applies it, and C's outbox row is acknowledged.
2. R's local owner runs `heimdall access resume --policy P` (or `access resume --all`) on a terminal.
3. The approval shows the policy action. `lifted_effects` is absent, so nothing says this releases C's delivered pause, delivered at T.
4. After the typed `yes`, P is active. The next report says C's revision is `in_force: false`.
5. A second owner controller D is no longer blocked by C's effect (the "Two owner controllers" rule), with no acknowledgement anywhere. C resumes once its own owner acknowledges.

The two keys still hold. What is missing is the runner owner being told whose effect they are lifting, which the ruling requires.

**Reproduction:** not reproduced end to end. A test for it would have to be added to the repository, which is read-only for this review. Observed parts:
- `cargo test -p heimdall-store --lib ext_effects::tests::the_report_says_which_delivered_effects_are_still_in_force` passes (1 passed). It asserts that clearing the policies' cutoffs through policy writes flips `in_force` to false. That is the implicit lift path.
- `cargo test -p heimdall-runtime --test integration_controller_driver_m06 a_lift_the_report_shows_reopens_issuance_only_once_it_is_acknowledged` passes (1 passed). Its runner-side lift is exactly the untargeted `Resume { policy_id: Some(policy_id()), target: None }` on a held policy (`tests/integration/controller_driver_m06.rs:663`), and it does not check `lifted_effects`.
- The naming tests cover only:
  - a policy-less runner (`tests/integration/runner_effects_m06.rs:1028`, `:1302`);
  - exact-scope `cutoff --all` in `exit_m06/lift.rs` and `t06.rs`.

The rest is code reading of the lines cited above.

**Suggested fix:**
- Compute the lifted effects for every attended widening resume or clear-cutoff: untargeted, targeted of any scope, and empty-set.
- Do it as `note_lifted_policy_revisions` would judge them after the change, so the decision view names each effect the approval will release.
- Or restrict the implicit clearing to effects the approval named.

### M06-W5-R02 (low): `route add` of an approved Iroh endpoint with a wider policy answers OK `already_present` and silently drops the requested exposure

**Where:**
- `crates/app/src/routes_ops.rs:363`: `already_present` matches `RouteSpec` only, not `exposure`.
- `crates/runtime/src/supervise_transport.rs:1634`: `add_precheck` short-circuits before any local record is made.

**Failure scenario:** a destination has an approved Iroh route to endpoint E under the minimal policy. The owner runs `heimdall route add lab --iroh E --relay https://relay.example/` on a terminal. It answers `OK`, `already_present: true`, with no decision. The relay is neither consented nor applied, and the answer does not say the requested policy differs. The owner may believe the relay is in use.

This is narrower than requested, so it is not a consent issue. `transport enable` answers `CONFIG_CONFLICT` for the same mismatch (`crates/app/src/transport.rs:598`).

**Reproduction (observed):** `cargo test -p heimdall-runtime --lib dispatch::tests::a_local_widening_is_recorded_and_approved_only_by_the_attended_local_owner` passes. It asserts this behaviour (`crates/runtime/src/dispatch.rs:3194`: `add("req_5", true, true)`, a relay added to the approved endpoint, gives `already_present == true`).

**Suggested fix:** answer `CONFIG_CONFLICT` naming the differing policy, as `transport enable` does. Or route a differing exposure through the widening path.

### M06-W5-R03 (low): changing the sshd port is refused, contrary to the ADR text

**Where:**
- `crates/app/src/transport.rs:598`: an enabled endpoint with any other policy is `CONFIG_CONFLICT`. The module docs say "`disable`, then `enable`".
- The ADR text: ADR-0012 `:142` and ADR-0006 §18.9 item 1 (`0006…md:2598`), "Changing the port is a narrowing-or-equal owner action, in the same transaction as the other serve settings."

**Failure scenario:** an owner serving on 22 runs `transport enable --serve --sshd-port 2222`. They get `CONFIG_CONFLICT` and must `disable`, which withdraws K_iroh references and consents, then make a new serve decision. That is stricter than the ruling. The delta binding is correct, since `sshd_port` is in the recorded `after` policy.

**Reproduction:** not run (it needs a running runtime with a K_iroh store). Code reading only. The CLI side was observed (probe 2 below).

**Suggested fix:** amend the ADR text to the built behaviour, or implement the in-place change.

### M06-W5-R05 (low): a relay URL's path can still carry a token, and the URL is echoed afterwards

**Where:** `crates/contract/src/route.rs:274-282`. Plain path segments of `[A-Za-z0-9/._~-]` are accepted.

**Failure scenario:** an operator pastes a relay URL that carries an access token in its path, for example `https://relay.example/t0k3n`. It is accepted, stored in the decision, the route and the consent, and quoted back:
- in the unattended `INTERACTION_REQUIRED` detail ("the relay …", `crates/runtime/src/local_widening.rs:108`);
- in `inspect decisions`.

ADR-0006 §18.9 item 9's aim is "no secret can arrive through it". As built, userinfo, any query and any fragment are refused (verified, probe 1). A path token is not.

**Reproduction:** code reading. Iroh relay URLs normally have no path, so this is a nit.

**Suggested fix:** accept only `/` as the path, unless a path is a requirement, or document the limit.

## Checked and found clean

### 1. The named-pipe agent

Files: `crates/credential/src/mgmt/agent.rs`, the `SshSessions::open` wiring in `m06_cmd.rs`, `heimdall_ipc::windows::Listener` (unchanged M04 code).

- **Name:** `\\.\pipe\heimdall-` plus 32 hex from the OS CSPRNG, nothing else. `is_session_pipe_name` refuses every other form before any pipe is created.
- **Creation:** first instance with `first_pipe_instance(true)`, so an existing name is refused, never joined. `reject_remote_clients(true)`, plus the computer-name check.
- **DACL:** `O:<user>D:P(A;;GA;;;<user>)`, built from this process's own SID whatever `owner` is.
- **Further instances:** need `FILE_CREATE_PIPE_INSTANCE`, which only the owner holds. The listener always keeps one waiting instance: `waiting` is never `None` after bind. So another account cannot interpose an instance during the session.
- **Client SID:**
  - The listener impersonates at identification level after the first read and compares `TokenUser` with the owner. `peer_admitted` checks once more: case-insensitive equality, and an empty SID never matches.
  - A wrong SID, a silent client or one past `MAX_SESSION_CONNECTIONS` (8) is disconnected unanswered.
- **Bounds:**
  - frames are bounded by `MAX_SIGNING_FRAME_BYTES`, checked from the header;
  - reads are bounded by the idle timeout;
  - connections are counted in a JoinSet, reaped first because of `biased`.
- **Scope:** the scope is `MgmtSigner`'s (unchanged since W2):
  - list returns K_mgmt only;
  - sign answers only a `publickey` userauth for the runner account (host-bound to the pinned host keys when host-bound);
  - every other type and extension answers `SSH_AGENT_FAILURE`, including `session-bind`, add, remove, lock and unknown types.
  - Nothing is forwarded. There is no upstream agent anywhere in the path.
- **Lifetime:**
  - `stop` and `Drop` both cancel. On Windows `Drop` aborts the task, which drops the JoinSet (so every connection), the listener (so every instance) and then the signer, whose key is zeroized on drop.
  - `SshSession::drop` drops the agent before the ssh child and removes the session directory.
  - Every refusal in `open` after the agent starts drops it.
- **Reparse:** a pipe name is not a file. The session directory is created exclusively with a protected owner-only DACL under a root that must not be a link. The files inside get the creator's default DACL, not world access. They hold no secrets: the public key, the pins and the config naming the pipe.
- **Same-user processes:**
  - Inference, not observed on Windows: named-pipe names are enumerable by any local process (`\\.\pipe\` listing). The token keeps the name unpredictable, which defeats pre-creation squatting, but does not make it secret during the session.
  - A same-user process can reach the agent. ADR-0009 treats such a process as a residual: it can already read the DPAPI blob itself, so it gains nothing.
  - The connected pipe client is the in-box `ssh.exe`, which is outside this code.
- **Observed:**
  - `cargo test -p heimdall-credential --lib mgmt::agent`: 6 passed (pipe-name form, peer check, scope over an in-memory stream, frame bounds, idle bound, Unix serve and unlink).
  - The `windows_tests` module (DACL, wrong SID, squatter, teardown, scope over the pipe, connection bound) is Windows-only and not run here. It is for the native run.

### 2. Local widening

Files: `crates/runtime/src/local_widening.rs`, `supervise_transport.rs` (`route`, `transport`), `remote/src/widening.rs`, `store/src/ext_m06.rs`, migration 0013, `dispatch.rs`.

- **Paired sessions:**
  - `submission_of` returns `Paired` whenever `remote_client` is set, so a paired widening falls to `ops.execute(..., None)`, which refuses `exposure_unconsented`.
  - No local record is ever created for a paired session. The record port cannot mark a paired request local, because `submit` and `submit_bound` always record the client.
  - Paired `Decide` is refused `local_owner_required` in `handle_as` before `decide_local`. `decide_local` re-checks remote, attended and owner.
- **Unattended local owner:** answered `INTERACTION_REQUIRED` with nothing written. A non-owner local caller is refused before anything.
- **Approval:**
  - Approval is bound to the recorded delta. The delta is recomputed now and compared before the claim, and again in `execute_local`.
  - The operations compare the approval's delta with their own (`consent_for`, and `add_inner`'s `a.delta == delta`).
  - It needs checked time, and is consumed once (`DECISION_STALE` after).
  - An authority-generation move stales it.
- **Retries:** idempotent by (kind, `local_digest` of the request without `request_id`, plus the delta), inside a `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` transaction. A lapsed or other-generation record is settled and replaced.
- **Store:** the CHECK constraint and the reading code both enforce the origin, client and digest agreement.
- **Executors:** serve on a runner, and `adopt-roost` and widening `route add` on a controller. They run only through `ApprovedWidening`. There is no other construction of `ApprovedWidening` in production code. I grepped every `TransportOps {` and `RouteOps {` site.
- **Prechecks** write nothing.
- **Relays:** `--relay` URLs are normalised before the delta, digest and lookup, on both the local and paired paths.
- **Observed:**
  - `cargo test -p heimdall-remote --lib widening`: 3 passed.
  - `cargo test -p heimdall-store --lib local_widening`: 2 passed.
  - `heimdall-runtime` lib, 12 passed, including:
    - `dispatch::tests::a_local_widening_is_recorded_and_approved_only_by_the_attended_local_owner`;
    - `…route_add_relay_urls_are_validated_and_normalised_before_any_record`;
    - `…a_lapsed_local_widening_shows_expired…`;
    - `…route_health_is_reported_only_by_the_binding_typed_and_bounded`;
    - the `runner::launch` tests;
    - `the_serve_target_is_the_policys_sshd_port_on_127_0_0_1_and_nothing_else`.

### 3. Lift, roles and the two-key rule

- **Barrier:** `lift_shown` now requires the effect's own revision row with `in_force: false`.
  - `in_force` defaults to `true` on the wire, so an older runner's report fails closed.
  - It is set to `true` on every apply, cleared only by `note_lifted…` or `lift_remote_policy_revision`, and sticky afterwards.
- **Second key:** the controller's own effect still needs `remote_lift_ack`, which is attended through `resume-issuance`.
- **Two owner controllers:** another controller's effect is filtered out only when the runner reports it lifted. In force, it blocks. This matches the ruling.
- **Requesters:**
  - `awaits_owner_record` makes a paired non-owner's ensure resolve every pair as Managed (fixed pairs included), never the runner's existing access.
  - Naming a CA is refused `issuer_untrusted` unless the trust entry names that client. Only active owners can be trusted as issuers (`runner.rs:454`).
  - The controller side refuses `pair_issuers` for a requester and records nothing.
- **Viewers:** read-only session forms.
- **Endpoint rotation:** `rotate_endpoint` on the base capability touches only the session's own row. It bumps `remote_authority_generation`, not the generation local records use.
- **`environment remove`:** revokes first (owner records), then forgets; limited records are only forgotten, and the output says so.
- **Runner-scoped policies:** an `access allow --environment <runner>` policy goes through the decision path and is restricted to one runner with an issuing role.

### 4. Runner launch hygiene

- **The clean launch:** `plan_launch` and `clean_environment` give:
  - exactly the declared pass-list (8 variables);
  - `HOME`, `USER`, `LOGNAME` and `SHELL` from `getpwuid_r(getuid())`, never the parent;
  - `LANG=C.UTF-8` when the parent has none;
  - a fixed `PATH`;
  - `SSH_AUTH_SOCK` only for a runner-managed endpoint under the runtime directory.
- **The parent's environment:** the CLI's delta unsets every parent variable. An inherited agent is reported by provenance, never by path. An override that would keep the caller's `SSH_AUTH_SOCK` is dropped.
- **`connect` on a runner:**
  - It adds the alias's explicit `IdentityAgent` endpoints as bound agents, so an unattended launch is refused `dependency_provenance_unknown` or `controller_dependency`.
  - An undetermined `IdentityAgent` (`~`, `%`, a leading `$`, `Match`, several values) is refused unattended. A `$` in the middle is treated as a literal path, which is still refused unattended as Unknown.
- **`run`:**
  - M02's unattended eligibility requires the alias's agent to equal the bound endpoint in every profile, and that endpoint then classifies Unknown, so the result is the same.
  - The undetermined check is wired at `run_cmd.rs:432`.
- **Starting the runtime from the bootstrap:** this path passes no `SSH_*` or agent variables (`RUNTIME_START_ENV`). It needs a typed `yes`, and descriptors above stdio are closed first.

### 5. Routes

- **`record_health`:**
  - Refused for paired sessions. The role table has it Unclassified, so it fails closed.
  - It requires the caller to be the very owner of the binding that holds the pair's slot at that endpoint (`same_caller`).
  - It is typed: routes from the stored approved set, a bounded duration, `host_pin_defeated` from the fixed set.
  - It is kept in memory, and is read only by `inspect destinations`. Nothing reorders on it today, and it cannot add or skip a pin.
- **`permit-port-forwarding`:**
  - It goes only to the bastion pair of a stored jump route, when both pairs are in the grant's scope and the bastion is a managed pair.
  - The destination never gains it, nor does a pair in another grant.
  - The test `a_stored_jump_route_gives_port_forwarding_to_its_bastion_only` covers this (read, not run).
- **The helper's checks:**
  - the static pin check over the pin settings;
  - the helper's own `ssh -G` check before any route;
  - the jump pins pre-checked, so a mismatch refuses the whole connect;
  - the same-grant check of the jump leg asked of the runtime per attempt;
  - `ssh -G` at `setup apply` only with `--allow-config-commands`.
- **The serve target:** fixed at `127.0.0.1:<sshd_port>`, `::1` refused, re-checked in the child. `sshd_port` is part of the consented `after` policy.
