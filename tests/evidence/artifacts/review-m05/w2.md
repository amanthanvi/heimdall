## Finding

### M05-W2-R01 — High; blocking: an in-flight child escapes a shared work stop

**Location:** [stop_m05.rs:817](crates/app/src/operations/stop_m05.rs:817) (`delegated_through`, especially lines 816–823), [stop_m05.rs:855](crates/app/src/operations/stop_m05.rs:855) (`work_plan`), and [operations.rs:8281](crates/app/src/operations.rs:8281) (child commit passes only the child's work identity to `activation_refusal`; the work-stop check is at [operations.rs:2193](crates/app/src/operations.rs:2193)).

**Concrete scenario:**

1. Jobs A and B make concurrent equivalent ensures and receive separate bindings on one grant.
2. A delegates a child with its own work ID. The child resolves and enters preparation; it has no committed binding yet.
3. The owner stops A. The stop records A's permanent work stop, releases A's binding, leaves B's reference usable, and successfully acknowledges `local_applied: true`.
4. The child's preparation finishes. It activates and a fresh bind returns `ACCESS_READY`, although it was delegated through A's stopped binding.
5. A second stop of A and an in-process restart still leave that child usable; another fresh bind returns `ACCESS_READY`.

On the shared-grant branch, the stop discovers children only if their grants already permit use **and** their committed bindings name A's binding as parent. A preparing child satisfies neither condition, so it is absent from the revoked set. Later activation checks the child's fresh work ID, which was never stopped. Its ancestor grant remains valid through B, so ancestor revalidation cannot detect the withdrawn parent-binding authority. A's own ID remains stopped; the child is the escape.

**Why this blocks under ADR-0009 §2.1:** this is an ordinary owner's stop racing an ordinary in-flight delegation, with no hostile same-user program required. The result is a durable active grant and usable binding after the owner withdrew that branch of authority. It violates ADR-0011 §7.2's requirement to revoke children delegated through the stopped work's bindings, and §7.3's prohibition on resurrection. The effect survives another stop and restart and can last to the child's deadline. It is outside the documented shared ordinary-alias residual: the child's own bound context remains usable.

**Executed probe:** [retained delegation probe](review-out/shared-stop-delegation-probe.rs) was temporarily appended to `tests/e2e/m05_stop.rs` and run through production `Operations`, the real SQLite store and the existing deterministic `FakeCredentialAdapter` pause. A/B sharing was obtained through actual concurrent ensures, rather than seeded reference rows. The probe checks the successful stop, A's released binding, B's active binding, the child's usable grant, and `ACCESS_READY` from fresh binds before and after restart.

- `cargo test --locked -p heimdall-runtime --test e2e_stop_m05 review_shared_work_stop_rejects_inflight_delegation`: **exit 101**, one assertion failure, after observing the child activate and bind successfully. [Original probe log](review-out/shared-stop-delegation-probe.log)
- A temporary commit-time fence that fails the child when its recorded parent binding belongs to stopped work: **exit 0**, one passed test. [Temporary fence log](review-out/shared-stop-delegation-temp-fence.log)
- Original production source restored byte for byte, same probe: **exit 101**, one assertion failure again. [Restored-source log](review-out/shared-stop-delegation-restored-source.log)
- Extended probe with repeat stop and restart: **exit 101**, one assertion failure at the final expected-refusal assertion; all preceding unsafe-state assertions passed. [Restart probe log](review-out/shared-stop-delegation-restart-probe.log)

**Smallest correction:** recheck the recorded parent binding's work stop in the transactions authorizing an admitted delegation's preparation/activation, including final commit and recovery. A child admitted before its parent work stopped must fail and settle its credential cleanup instead of becoming usable. Keep the existing treatment of live descendants and B's reference. Extend the shared-work stop regression with this paused-child interleaving. The temporary commit-only fence proved the missing check is causal; it was removed and is not offered as a complete retained implementation.

## 1. Unattended widening

No additional out-of-policy widening path was found in U3/U4/U7. Grants and bindings created without a new attended decision derive previously approved standing authority or a parent's subset authority; creating one is not itself a new standing permission. R01 is the exception to withdrawal enforcement: a previously admitted delegation can activate after the authority of its parent binding has been withdrawn.

Targeted narrowing resolves its target inside the applying transaction. Targeted widening records the widened policies and their revisions. [stop_m05::listed](crates/app/src/operations/stop_m05.rs:664) rejects a missing/revised listed policy; [stop_m05::apply](crates/app/src/operations/stop_m05.rs:743) iterates that stored list rather than resolving the target again. A newly matching policy cannot silently join the approval. Existing staleness, partial narrowing/widening and uncertain-time tests passed.

Context admission stores a server-built resolution. Trusted-work deltas use attended `WidenPolicy` decisions; trust/reconcile actions keep observation bases. No manifest value becomes consent. Import writes new catalog destinations/profiles only, as approved by revision 5.1. These definitions are intent, not automatic activation permission: import cannot enlarge an existing policy and does not replace existing profiles. The integration regression also checks a policy naming a previously undefined imported profile: only its saved subset activates; the added pair still needs a decision.

## 2. Stops and I-1

**R01 must be fixed.** Ordinary tested stop/pause/cutoff behavior passed: pause refuses new grants/bindings while existing bindings continue; cutoff bounds continuing use; policy stop pauses and revokes its resting grants and descendants; resume and clear-cutoff change only their own block, and neither revives terminal grants. Shared work stop releases only the stopped work's committed bindings and leaves other work usable.

Fresh requests for the stopped work ID, binds of its released binding, and delegations naming that stopped ID are refused. The independently executed work-stop revert proof made a fresh request reactivate that ID, and restoration refused it again. The uncovered case is a child with a different work ID admitted through the parent **before** the stop.

I-1's [reusable_grant](crates/app/src/operations/stop_m05.rs:1302) filters active nondelegated bindings in the target environment, requires a usable nondelegated standing-policy grant, and compares scope, effective deadline, profile/revision, mechanism, caller, origin workspace and binding context. It then revalidates through `Activation::NewBinding`. It cannot directly attach personal work to a workspace grant, another workspace's grant, another environment, or differing pins. Flight coalescing and waiting-decision joining also compare workspace context. The independent context-coalescing revert proof demonstrated the regression those guards prevent.
